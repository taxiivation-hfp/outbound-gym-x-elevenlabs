Why we built this

The call you shouldn’t make

There is a gym near us with about 1,200 members and one person on the front desk. They can already see who has stopped coming — the report is in the club system, and it is not wrong. Nobody rings anyone, because ringing sixty people is a day nobody has, and tomorrow there are sixty more.

So we started where anyone would: automate the calls. Then we spent a week with the data and found the part nobody wants to say out loud.

What everyone builds

Two halves of the same assumption

One half of the market predicts. Keepme, PredictStay and Glofox’s at-risk report will all score your members and hand you a list. The list is the product; what happens next is your problem.

The other half executes. Replify and Keepme’s Antares will make the calls at volume, and contact rate is the number they report back, because a call placed is the only thing they can prove happened.

Both halves assume the same thing: that contact is neutral. That the worst a call can do is nothing. Point either of them at the same data and they will ring every name on it.

What we found

A call is not free

Phoning someone who has drifted reminds them they are paying for something they are not using. For plenty of members that is a useful nudge. For a particular kind of member — the one who was never really regular, who has not thought about the direct debit in four months — it is the reminder that ends the membership. You did that. The report said they were at risk, and the call is what made it true.

So the first thing this thing predicts is not who is about to leave. It is who you would lose by getting in touch — and then it leaves them alone.

On the gym we built it against, that is 145 members out of 500 who are never called. Not deferred, not queued for later, not tried once to see what happens. They sit in the same table as everyone else, below a divider, with the reason written next to them — cooling off, in a billing dispute, asked us to stop, or simply too quiet for a call to help. A manager can see exactly who is being protected and why, and override it. It just is not the default.

That is the whole product, really. Everything below this is in service of it.

How it works

Four conversations, not one

A member whose contract ends on Friday and a member who lapsed in May need nothing in common from a phone call, so they do not share an agent. There are four, and each one only knows what it needs to.

Each gym types in what it actually offers — the discount, the cheaper plan, the freeze, whether classes even need booking — and the wording the agent reads is generated from that. A blank field means the gym did not say, which is not the same as no. No gym gets a script we wrote for a different gym.

The arithmetic

Forty-one cents

A call costs us $0.41. A saved member is worth $473 to the gym. Break-even is 0.09% — one save in roughly 1,200 calls, which is a bar so low it is almost embarrassing to state.

That is exactly why the restraint has to be built in rather than left to judgement. When calling is this cheap, nothing in the economics will ever stop you calling everybody. The only discipline left is the decision not to, and it has to live in the product, because it will not survive a quarterly target.
